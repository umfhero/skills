# Register and author voice

These are defaults, not a claim to reproduce one universal human voice. Use the user's supplied samples and explicit preferences when available. Do not infer that a construction is forbidden because it is absent from a small sample.

| Register | Aim | Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dissertation or formal academic | Build a qualified argument from evidence; explain how a finding supports a conclusion. | Citations, technical distinctions, uncertainty, limitations and discipline-appropriate terminology. |
| Report or coursework | Explain the work, decisions and consequences in relation to the brief. | Requirements, results and the distinction between completed and proposed work. |
| Professional email | Make the purpose and next action clear in courteous, direct language. | Who is asking whom for what; deadlines and commitments only when provided. |
| Casual-professional post | Explain the work as someone familiar with it would explain it to a peer. | Supported first person, natural transitions, technical shorthand appropriate to the reader. |
| Casual planning | Use plain, relaxed language and a useful sequence of actions. | Actual preferences and unresolved choices; brevity where it helps. |

Prefer complete, connected sentences. Let length follow the thought. Do not expand “The build failed.” solely to meet a clause quota, and do not split a careful explanation into dramatic fragments.

When samples are available, infer a few tendencies: directness, typical connectors, level of technical explanation and paragraph development. Apply only the relevant tendencies. Preserve an intentional informality without deliberately creating comma splices, misspellings or run-ons.

Do not import an author's project names, personal circumstances or experiences into unrelated drafts. Keep private source samples out of a shared skill package unless the user explicitly chooses to publish them.

Compare the finished prose with the task, not an imagined detector. Naturalness comes from a clear purpose, credible substance and appropriate expression; no single punctuation pattern proves it.
